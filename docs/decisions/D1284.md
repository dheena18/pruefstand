[T5.4e full run, plan approved by the lead; implements D1242 step 4] `experiments/rq2/vm_session_run.py` runs one resumable session of the VM judge. A session is repeated until it prints `done`.

**Start.**
- It refuses (exit 75) in the night window, less than 30 minutes before 22:00 Europe/Berlin, or when the stop file `~/pruefstand/rq2-vm.stop` exists.
- It starts `vm_llm.sh start 14b` with `VM_LLM_MAX_MIN` = the session cap, so the server itself dies at the cap. `vm_llm.sh` takes `pruefstand-lock` and every check slot, and refuses while either is busy or while `MAINTENANCE` is set (D1285, #244). `finally` always stops the server, and SIGTERM unwinds into it.

**Scoring and resume.**
- It scores the arm's calls one at a time, through the RQ2 judge disk cache, in `run_judges` order (items, then both sides of each degraded pair).
- So the next session skips every cached call. A killed session loses at most the call in flight.
- When everything is cached, `run_judges.py` writes the final parquets (all cache hits).

**Stopping before each call.** The session stops cleanly on the first of these:
1. The deadline: the session cap of **100 minutes × 0.9** (90 minutes of calls), or 21:55 Europe/Berlin, whichever comes first.
2. The stop file.
3. **Lock wanted:** a blocked waiter (`->`) in `/proc/locks` on any lock that our holder processes hold.

**The cap is the real protection (review of #243).** The gate orchestrator (`gate_orchestrator.lock`) and `vm_check.sh` wait for a lock by polling with `LOCK_NB` / `flock -n`, so they never appear as blocked waiters (`->`) in `/proc/locks`, and the lock-wanted check cannot see them. A session is therefore capped below the gate job's lock wait (`gate_job.LOCK_TIMEOUT_S`, 2 h); a test fails if the cap is not below it. A gate job dispatched during a session gets the lock within at most about 100 minutes and never times out as "runner busy". At 30 s/call, a session holds about 180 calls:
- 150 per set: about 874 calls (four sets + MEMERAG 274), 5 sessions;
- 300 per set: about 1 474 calls, 9 sessions;
- `--pairs` adds to both.

The `/proc/locks` check stays as a defence against blocking waiters such as `flock` without `-n`.

**How lock wanted is detected.**
- The holder processes are the `llama-server` pid from `vm_llm.sh`'s PID file and its ancestors up to pid 1. That chain includes the `pruefstand-lock`/flock launcher, so a waiter for `gpu.lock` (a gate job) or for a check slot (`make check-vm`) both count.
- `/proc/locks` is the only lock source. Agents never touch `/var/lib/pruefstand/**`, not even with `stat`.
- When no line of our holders is found, the session stops (fail safe).

The driver does not check `MAINTENANCE` itself, for the same reason. It **depends on** `vm_llm.sh start` refusing while `MAINTENANCE` exists (D1285, #244). The full run does not start before #244 is merged. Sessions are logged to `data/derived/rq2/vm_sessions.jsonl`, which is git-ignored.
