[T0.3 follow-up, flaky `test_stack_run_signalled_tears_down_and_leaves_no_process`; `mk/docker.mk` `stack-run`, `scripts/tests/test_docker_mk.py` and the new `scripts/tests/test_docker_mk_stack_run.py`; no interface change] Root causes found by looping the scenario under CPU stress (before: 4 failures in 960 group-TERM runs, all with `down` never run; 40 of 40 make-only TERM runs left CMD running): (a) the recipe ran under `sh` (dash), where `set -m` fails without a tty, so CMD stayed in the caller's process group, `kill -TERM -$pid` signalled nothing and CMD survived a TERM sent to make alone (the old test hid this: it blocked on the pipe until CMD's `sleep 30` ended); (b) make forwards TERM to its child and a group signal delivers a second one; the second TERM ran the `exit 143` trap inside the EXIT trap and aborted `down` (the observed flake); (c) the group-kill trap was installed after the fork of CMD; (d) a signal reaching the new group while its leader is between fork and exec is lost. Fix: the lock command is `bash -c` (job control works without a tty); because CMD now has its own process group, TERM, INT, HUP and QUIT are all trapped (a closed SSH or VS Code terminal sends HUP to the group; untrapped, CMD would outlive make and hold `stack.lock`); every handler first ignores further signals, and `down` inherits that ignore, so a hung `down` needs KILL from outside; the handler is installed before CMD is forked, a signal between `&` and `pid=$!` is replayed via `stop`, and CMD starts with default handlers (`trap -`); the handler re-sends TERM every 0.2 s until the group is gone and falls back to KILL after 2 s. GNU make forwards only TERM to its children, so INT/HUP/QUIT are tested as group signals only. Tests (34 collected on main, 40 now): the signal test (7 cases: TERM to make, TERM to the group, TERM x3, INT, HUP, QUIT, and a CMD that ignores TERM) waits for `started <pgid>` on a reader thread with deadlines (no blocking pipe read, no fixed sleep), checks CMD's own process group and that `stack.lock` is free, and bounds signal-to-exit time far below CMD's 120 s; the stack-run tests moved to the new file (300-line limit). Separately, two `make -n stack-run` tests ran the real `flock` on the shared `~/pruefstand/stack.lock` (`-n` still runs recipe lines containing `$(MAKE)`) and hung whenever another agent held a stack session; they now use a temporary lock. `bash` is already required by the repo scripts.
