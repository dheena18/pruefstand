[T3.1b, reconciles D655 with D1286; supersedes D1286's numbers, not its conclusion] **The figure to rely on is D655's: 89.4 min for a full `core` PR run, and 62.1 min with the 150-case judged subset, at the 256-token cap.** D1286's 98.8 / 67.9 min are too high.

**Why the two numbers differ.** Both count 350 generations (smoke 50 + core 300), 256 output tokens, prefill 966 tok/s and decode 19.4 tok/s on one slot. `budget.report()` differed from `scenarios()` (D655) in two stale phase-1 inputs:
- **Prompt tokens:** 3 500 instead of the measured tier-2 prompt of about 1 650 tokens (load log 2026-11-03, D653).
  - That is 350 × (3 500 − 1 650) / 966 s = **+11.2 min** of prefill: 16.82 s instead of 14.91 s per generation.
- **Embedding:** 0.05 s instead of the measured 0.19 s per query.
  - That is 790 × 0.14 s = **−1.8 min**.

98.8 − 11.2 + 1.8 = **89.4**.

**Fix.** `budget.report()` now uses the measured inputs from `scenarios.py` (`MEASURED_PROMPT_TOKENS`, `MEASURED_EMBED_S`). A test fails if `report()` and `scenarios()` disagree at the 256 cap.

**What does not change.**
- With D655's 20 % margin (72 min), full `core` does not fit at 89.4 min either. The **subset stays the PR default** and is mandatory, not optional: 62.1 min, 31 % margin.
- The nightly is 149 min, not D1286's 168.
- The reranker verdict of D1287 is unaffected. The reranker runs on the CPU, in parallel with GPU0.
