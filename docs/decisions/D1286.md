[T3.1b S5, budget from the measured rates; input to D199 / R13 D295] With the generator's real output cap the PR tier no longer fits 90 minutes on its full `core` run. The **150-case judged subset is needed** unless the generator gets faster.

**Inputs.**
- The generator's output tokens come from `generation.max_tokens` in `apps/rag-demo/configs/rag.gpu.yaml`: **256**, not the phase-1 assumption of 200.
- The rates are the 2026-11-03 phase-1 measurements (`experiments/model_selection/REPORT.md`):
  - generator: 1 slot (it serialises), 19.4 tok/s decode, 966 tok/s prefill, 3 500 prompt tokens, 350 generations;
  - judge: 4 slots, 25.6 tok/s per stream, 1 625 tok/s prefill, 3 000 prompt and 150 output tokens, 900 calls;
  - embedding: 0.05 s per query;
  - rerank: 1.2 s/query, **assumed** until `rerank_bench.py` measures it on the VM CPU.

**Result** (`budget.report()`).

| Run | GPU0 | GPU1 | CPU (rerank) | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PR, full core | **98.8 min** | 49.7 | 15.8 | 90 min: over |
| PR, 150-case subset | 67.9 min | 24.8 | 15.8 | fits |
| Nightly, 600 generations | 168.2 min | 99.3 | – | 480 min: fits |

GPU0 binds: the generator serialises. The 200-token phase-1 estimate was 81.9 min.

**Consequence.** By D199 and R13 D295, PR runs use `--judged-subset` (generation and judging of the `core` answerable cases limited to the 150-case subset; security cases in full) when T3.1b measures more than 90 minutes. That is the case here, on these inputs. The final word is T6.3's measured PR-tier timing (`results/noise/timings.csv`) on the real tier-2 stack. Ways to recover the full `core` run:
- a faster generator setting (more slots, if the load log shows they help);
- a lower `generation.max_tokens`;
- a shorter prompt budget.

Each of these is a separate decision; none is taken here.
